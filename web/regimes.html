Rule (quanty.regime.v1, fixed parameters): bull when the daily close is above its 200-day average and the 50-day average is above the 200-day; bear when both are below; chop when they disagree. A switch needs the new state on 3 consecutive days. High vol when the 30-day realized volatility is above 1.5× its median over the previous 365 days. No look-ahead: each day's label uses bars up to that day only, and a call takes the label of the last daily bar that had closed before it was posted.

Score-neutral: the main caller score does not change. The per-regime numbers use the same rules (hit rate, return quality, clarity, shrinkage n/(n+10) toward 50) on the calls made in each regime, so most cells are small samples. The regime is the crypto market's (BTC) for every call, including stocks and commodities (24 calls are not crypto: read their cells with that in mind).

Where it degrades

  • Lag at turns. Moving averages lag: the regime leaves bull or bear only after price has moved away from the top or bottom (recent flips below). A call made in the first weeks after a top is still labelled bull.
  • Whipsaws in sideways markets. 16 runs of 30 days or less between two runs of the same regime (2020 and Aug-Oct 2024 most of all); calls made then sit near the boundary, and the stability table shows they are the ones that move when a parameter moves.
  • Calls are not spread over regimes. 40 of 59 calls were made in bull; every bear-regime call is by @BobLoukas, so the bear column is one caller's record, not a comparison.
  • One market for every asset. The regime is BTC's for every call, including the 24 stock and commodity calls, whose own market can differ.
  • Data. Binance's 2018-02-08 daily bar is a truncated outage bar, so the series starts on 2018-02-09; the first label needs 200 + 2 days of warm-up (2018-08-29). A stored bar that changes, a gap, a duplicate or a stale tail is refused, and a call the data does not reach is labelled unknown, never with an old label.
