The Fed Is A Sideshow While The AI Trade Implodes | Weekly Roundup

Listen to the original episode. Below: what was said, in our words, then every stance on a tradable asset with its timestamp.

1 · The episode in short

  1. 2:37 The hosts review the Fed pause and its two dissents and the plan to hold fewer meetings. Quinn calls the Fed a boxed-in sideshow that cannot hike amid AI, oil-supply and growth risks.
  2. 8:44 They discuss single-mandate central banks: the ECB and Bank of Canada are hiking into an energy shock, partly to defend their currencies. Quinn expects the dollar to peak and roll over within weeks to months as the Fed is forced to ease.
  3. 13:58 Gold is making new lows despite falling yields, blamed on a liquidity crunch and energy-short sovereigns selling reserves. Both say the fundamentals are good, but they would step aside until it regains the summer lows.
  4. 19:13 Felix argues the AI trade is fracturing because Microsoft and Google capex guidance came in below estimates, while GDP growth leans on the AI buildout. Quinn adds that higher real rates, wider credit spreads and leverage unwinding point to a long, cyclical bust in semis.
  5. 33:03 They separate the usefulness of AI from its financing. Quinn argues that compute becoming commoditized is bearish for margins and says he is not a semis buyer until perhaps 2027.
  6. 40:00 Quinn makes his oil case: prices are rising on supply disruption during a pre-midterm peace, positioning is light, and he expects escalation after the midterms. He advocates being long early-2027 oil contracts.
  7. 43:28 They discuss inflation as an invisible tax, voter frustration, protests in Paris, midterm outlooks and unreliable polling, and what a win or loss could mean for markets.
  8. 53:42 The $6B Binance hack makes Felix reconsider his crypto bullishness. Quinn holds no position and expects better conditions next year, likely after gold bottoms.
