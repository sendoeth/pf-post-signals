The Fed Is A Sideshow While The AI Trade Implodes | Weekly Roundup

1 · The episode in short

  1. 2:46 The Fed held with two hawkish dissents. Quinn calls the meeting meaningless: the Fed cannot hike into an AI unwind, an oil supply shock and slowing growth, so December likely comes off the table while inflation keeps rising.
  2. 8:52 Single-mandate central banks (ECB, Bank of Canada) keep hiking into an energy shock, mostly to defend their currencies. Quinn ties this to gold: energy-short nations sell gold to defend currency while the dollar is strong, but expects the dollar to peak and roll over within weeks to months.
  3. 13:58 Gold broke below 3,900 to a one-year low even with bonds rallying. Both see forced selling (liquidity crunch, sovereigns). Long-run case intact, but step aside below the summer lows until it recovers them.
  4. 19:21 The AI trade is fracturing: Microsoft and Google capex guidance missed estimates (38B vs 45B, 51B vs 55B). Credit spreads widening, neocloud credit window shut, leveraged products unwound. Quinn shorts pops and expects a long bleed; maybe a first bullish semis call in 2027.
  5. 30:46 Oil-industry lesson: over-levered producers keep producing into a bust, so a supply glut lasts. The same applies to chips and data centers. Compute becoming a commodity is bearish for margins even if the technology is great.
  6. 39:50 Quinn's main call: long oil, early-2027 futures expiries. Prices rose through three months of pre-midterm peace with almost no positioning; the supply disruption forces US action after the midterms.
  7. 44:35 Inflation as an invisible tax feeding populism, unrest and anti-data-center politics; prediction markets missed Brazil's election.
  8. 53:48 The $6.36B Binance hack dents crypto sentiment. Felix is less constructive; Quinn is not long anything and sees no fertile conditions until next year. Bottom-ish signal, but time capitulation first; gold's new lows could be ominous for Bitcoin.
